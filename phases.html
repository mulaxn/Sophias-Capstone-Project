The Menstrual Cycle: Phases & Symptoms

A typical cycle runs about 28 days (this varies by person) and has four phases. Hormone levels — mainly estrogen and progesterone — rise and fall across these phases, which is what drives changes in energy, mood, and physical symptoms.

1. Menstrual Phase (Day 1–~5)

The start of the follicular phase. Estrogen and progesterone are both low. Common symptoms: cramps, fatigue, and general discomfort.

2. Follicular Phase (~Day 1–13)

Estrogen gradually rises as follicles develop in the ovary. Many people notice an improvement in mood and energy levels during this window.

3. Ovulation (~Day 14)

Estrogen peaks just before ovulation. A surge in luteinizing hormone (LH) triggers the release of an egg. Some people experience pelvic pain.

4. Luteal Phase (~Day 15–28)

Progesterone rises, along with some estrogen. Common symptoms: bloating, increased appetite, mood changes, and fatigue.

Cycle length and symptoms vary widely between individuals — this is a general guide, not a prediction of how any one person will feel. Source: Schmalenberger et al. (2020).
